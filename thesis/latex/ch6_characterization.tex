\chapter{How Prompts Move Each Model}
\label{ch:char}

Having established a trustworthy way to measure prompts (Chapter~\ref{ch:proxybias}) and used
it to find good ones (Chapter~\ref{ch:repair}), we characterize \emph{how} the two knobs move
each model. The exhaustive grid lets us report clean marginals rather than the confounded
trial-correlations of an adaptively-sampled search.

\section{One universal lever, one that mostly costs safety}

Averaging over the other axis, one step up the safety scale raises Harm Avoidance by about
$+12$ points on every model, at a Control-Pragmatism cost of $-7$ to $-12$. One step up the
goal-pressure scale \emph{lowers} HA by 3 to 6 points while buying only $+1.5$ to $+3.9$ CP
(Figure~\ref{fig:marginals}). The safety knob is a reliable, universal control on harm
avoidance; goal pressure is largely a safety eroder whose pragmatism benefit is small.

The controlled dose--response at neutral safety framing (Table~\ref{tab:dose}) makes the
asymmetry concrete. Increasing pressure from none to strong costs 52 points of HA on Llama and
7.5 on Gemini, while CP rises materially ($+15.8$) only on Llama---the one model with CP
headroom at neutral framing ($81 \to 97$); the others already sit at 96--99. The common recipe
``add goal pressure to make the agent more effective'' is a poor trade almost everywhere.

An earlier version of this project, using trial-correlations from the biased-proxy runs,
reported that goal pressure ``drives pragmatism on Llama but is inert on Mistral.'' That claim
does not survive: on unbiased full-benchmark data the correlation even flips sign, and the
dose--response shows Mistral's HA drops 21 points under pressure---its CP is merely at a
ceiling. The apparent inertness was a ceiling effect plus a confounded estimator, a direct
example of why Chapter~\ref{ch:proxybias}'s correction matters for the science.

\section{The ROC lens: steerability is discrimination quality}

The two metrics are best understood together as an operating point on a classifier. Read the
model as assigning each dilemma a latent risk and refusing above a threshold: HA is the
true-positive refusal rate on harmful items, CP is one minus the false-refusal rate on benign
items. Then the measured frontier is the model's \emph{ROC curve for harm discrimination},
hypervolume is its area-under-curve analogue, and a prompt selects a threshold along a fixed
curve. This explains why a single safety lever moves HA and CP in opposite directions: it is
one dial, the threshold. It also reframes steerability: a model holding high CP under a strict
HA floor (Qwen: CP 92 at $\HA \geq 50$) has better underlying separation of harmful from
benign items than one that collapses (Mistral); prompting cannot improve the curve, only choose
where to sit on it.

\section{What transfers and what does not}

\paragraph{Stakes wording (transfers).} ManagerBench's benefit and harm percentages are text
substituted into otherwise identical items, so a sweep over them tests sensitivity to the
\emph{stated magnitude} of stakes, not different questions. The grid winners move by at most 3.3
MB across the four benefit/harm slices.

\paragraph{Items (transfers).} Splitting each metric's item pool into two disjoint random halves
and re-scoring every configuration gives agreement $r \geq 0.989$ (MAE $\leq 2.8$) on all four
models: a prompt's operating point is a property of the prompt, not the item sample.

\paragraph{Models (does not transfer).} Prompts do not port across models. Qwen's strong-safety
prompt drives Llama to HA 100 / CP 14.6; Llama's weak-safety prompt drives every other model to
HA 3--13 / CP ${\approx}98$. Per-model tuning is required.

\section{Levels are not semantics (a negative result)}

The knobs are interpretable, but their strength is limited by a wording-sensitivity result we
state prominently. We wrote three human paraphrases of each winner's safety sentence, each
intended to preserve its semantic level, and re-evaluated the winners. The operating point
moved by whole tiers on three of four models: on Gemini a balanced 70/71 prompt became a
near-total refuser (97/6) under a level-preserving rewrite (CP $-37$ to $-65$); Mistral's HA
moved $-13$ to $-28$; Qwen's CP up to $-33$; only Llama was stable (within $\pm 8$). These
shifts exceed the distance between adjacent safety levels, so this is surface-form sensitivity,
not level drift. The knob abstraction therefore indexes \emph{specific template strings}, and
every guarantee in this thesis attaches to those frozen strings. As a standalone finding it is a
caution for practice: prompt-based safety steering is wording-fragile, and a deployment must
freeze and regression-test the exact prompt text.

\section{Frontiers, hypervolume, and operating points}

Collecting each model's measured configurations in the $(\HA, \CP)$ plane yields a clear
empirical frontier (Figure~\ref{fig:fronts}). On the exhaustive grid the hypervolume ordering
(reference $(0,0)$, maximum 10{,}000) is Qwen 8{,}320 $>$ Llama 7{,}511 $>$ Gemini 6{,}909 $>$
Mistral 6{,}823. Notably, Mistral's exhaustive-grid hypervolume (6{,}823) far exceeds the
5{,}255 obtained from the hand-crafted spectrum alone: its ``poor steerability'' in earlier
reporting was an artifact of the spectrum never sampling its good region (moderate safety, light
pressure)---exactly the region Chapter~\ref{ch:repair}'s winners occupy.
