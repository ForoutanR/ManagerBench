\chapter{Search Damage and Repair}
\label{ch:repair}

Chapter~\ref{ch:proxybias} established that an informativeness-selected proxy mis-estimates
full-benchmark scores. This chapter shows the consequence that makes the result matter:
scored by that proxy, a black-box optimizer returns prompts that lose to hand-written
baselines on every model---and repairing only the evaluation reverses the outcome.

\section{The misled search}

We ran 25-trial Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) optimization per model, scoring each
candidate on the discrimination proxy of Chapter~\ref{ch:proxybias}. Three facts about these
runs, each verified from the saved trial records, explain what went wrong. First, the intended
warm start from the hand-crafted spectrum never executed: every run's records show zero
warm-start trials and identical first-trial coordinates across models, i.e.\ a cold start from
a fixed seed. Second, the two-knob space decodes to only 45 distinct prompts (a step function
of 9 safety levels $\times$ 5 pressure levels); the 25 cold trials visited only 16--18 of the
45 cells per model, and for two models the cell containing the true optimum was never sampled.
Third, where the search did visit a good cell, the biased proxy mis-ranked it: on Mistral, the
truly-best cell (full-benchmark MB 57.3) scored 41.8--44.3 on the proxy, while the empty-prompt
cell (true MB 37.1) scored an inflated 62.9.

The result is that the optimizer's returned optima lose to each model's best hand-crafted
prompt on all four models (Table~\ref{tab:repair}). Worse, by decode identity, three of the
four returned prompts are byte-for-byte identical to existing spectrum prompts; their apparent
gains over those variants were re-measurement noise (re-running an identical prompt moves HA by
up to 4.7 points and CP by up to 5.6).

\section{The repair}

We changed only the evaluation, not the model, the space, or the optimizer family:
(i)~representative (difficulty-stratified) proxy selection; (ii)~a scoring convention counting
unparseable answers as wrong, matching the full benchmark (the live evaluator had previously
excluded them, a second source of over-estimation); and (iii), because the space is only 45
cells, an exhaustive sweep of every cell on every model. The grid cost \$4.64 in total API
spend (\$0.026 per cell), was checkpointed after every cell to survive connection blackouts,
and completed without incident.

For each model we validated the grid's argmax cell(s) on the full benchmark.

The repaired pipeline beats the best hand-crafted prompt on three of four models, by $+3.9$ to
$+16.7$ MB, and against the biased-proxy BO it gains $+7.7$ to $+36.9$. On Llama it falls 2.8
short: Llama's true optimum is itself a hand-crafted cell, the grid's top-two cells bracket it,
but the single proxy-argmax pick landed just below---the reason we recommend validating the
top-$k$ cells rather than only the top one. Every winning prompt occupies the same region---a
clear safety instruction with \emph{no or light} goal pressure---that neither the hand-crafted
spectrum (whose safety tiers were all tested under strong pressure) nor the biased search ever
sampled.

Two supporting measurements confirm the winners are trustworthy. The stratified proxy tracks
the full benchmark \emph{live}: on the 11 cells per model with both measurements, HA MAE is
2.2--3.3 and CP MAE 1.8--4.3 ($r \geq 0.99$), and the winner cells' proxy-to-full gaps are
0.2--5.2 MB. And three fresh proxy re-runs of each winner give a win-or-tie against the model's
best hand-crafted prompt of 3/3 on all three winning models (0/3 on Llama, consistent), with a
replication standard deviation of at most 2.8 MB.

\section{Is the optimizer even necessary?}

Because we measured the whole grid, we can ask what any optimizer would have achieved by
replaying it: draw noisy observations ($\sigma=3$, matching the ${\sim}90$-item binomial noise)
from the measured cells and report simple regret against the true grid optimum. Random search
is a strong baseline in a 45-cell space (regret $\leq 1.6$ MB at 30 evaluations on every
model); GP-BO helps decisively only where the good region is narrow (Llama: regret 0.7 at 15
evaluations versus 3.9 for random); UCB1 is uniformly mediocre. The efficiency of this pipeline
comes from the cheap, trustworthy proxy, not from optimizer sophistication---and a neural-bandit
optimizer, the primary method of the original proposal, cannot be justified at this scale.

\section{The claim}

The same benchmark, the same class of optimizer, and the same order of API budget produce
opposite verdicts depending only on how the cheap evaluation subset is chosen: under
informativeness selection the optimizer loses to hand-writing on 4/4 models; under
representative selection it wins on 3/4. \textbf{Proxy design does not merely distort reported
numbers---it can flip the sign of the optimization result.}
