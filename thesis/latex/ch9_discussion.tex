\chapter{Discussion, Limitations, and Future Work}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{What the results mean}

The through-line of this thesis is a single, somewhat uncomfortable claim: for safety-relevant
prompt optimization, the cheap evaluation subset is part of the optimizer, not a neutral
measurement of it. The same benchmark, optimizer class, and budget produce opposite conclusions
depending only on how the subset is chosen. The failure comes from the most natural selection
rule in the field (keep the informative items) and recurs in the decision-boundary component of
current performance-guided methods. Because the objective here is precisely a model's harm
behaviour, a silent bias in the evaluation is a safety-relevant defect that can certify the
wrong prompt.

The positive results are equally a story about measurement. Once the evaluation is
representative and, at this scale, exhaustive, the same pipeline finds prompts that beat careful
hand-writing on three of four models, in a region hand-tuning never explored; a model that
looked barely steerable turns out to have been under-sampled; and the map is regular enough to
invert into a controller with a distribution-free guarantee. None of this required a more
sophisticated optimizer---a replay comparison shows random search is competitive in a 45-cell
space---only a trustworthy proxy.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scope.} Four mid-sized hosted models, one benchmark, one decode template. Findings
are about the \emph{method} and \emph{per-model} behaviour, not a universal prompt.
\item \textbf{A small, discrete space.} The 45-cell decode makes exhaustive measurement possible
but the search problem easy, so optimizer-efficiency claims are modest by construction; larger or
learned spaces re-open the search question our replay closes here.
\item \textbf{Wording fragility.} The knobs index specific template strings, not paraphrase
classes: level-preserving rewrites shift the operating point by whole tiers on three of four
models. Every guarantee attaches to the frozen strings.
\item \textbf{Cold-start proxy.} Representative selection needs per-item difficulty estimates,
i.e.\ some prior full-benchmark responses; a brand-new model must be bootstrapped at real cost.
\item \textbf{Statistical caution.} The conformal calibration uses only 11 cells per model,
forcing conservative intervals that mid-frontier configurations occasionally exceed; the noise
floor (identical prompts re-measuring within $\Delta\HA \approx 4.7$) bounds single-point claims;
and the Llama result shows a single proxy-argmax pick can miss a near-tied optimum.
\item \textbf{External validity.} The operating points are defined on ManagerBench; the external
checks (\S\ref{sec:external}) test only selected prompts, with LLM-judge HarmBench scoring.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future work}

The natural next step follows from the classifier/threshold lens of Chapter~\ref{ch:char}. If a
prompt selects a threshold on a fixed harm-discrimination curve, the way to improve deployments
is to (i)~enlarge the prompt space along axes the two knobs cannot express---tone, role, output
format, and paraphrase variants treated as an explicit robustness dimension---and (ii)~trace the
resulting, no-longer-enumerable frontier with genuine multi-objective Bayesian optimization. The
right tool is a hypervolume-greedy, noise-aware acquisition such as qNEHVI
\citep{daulton2020ehvi} (observation noise set to the measured proxy variance), falling back to
random-scalarization ParEGO where dependencies must stay light; the stratified proxy, top-$k$
full-benchmark validation, and conformal wrapping carry over unchanged. Two further directions:
testing whether prompt-induced threshold shifts transfer to an external agentic benchmark
\citep{odcvbench2025}, upgrading external validity from a spot-check to a claim; and a
preference-conditioned controller \citep{panacea2024} that amortizes the inverse map into a
single learned function from requested trade-off to prompt, of which the present non-parametric
controller is the tabular special case. Finally, the cold-start limitation invites a
multi-fidelity bootstrap: spend a small full-benchmark budget to seed the difficulty estimates a
representative proxy needs, then run cheaply thereafter.

\section{A note on the proposal}

The approved proposal framed the task as constrained optimization with a neural-bandit
optimizer, an external harm benchmark as the constraint, and a local white-box model. The
core---budgeted black-box prompt optimization on ManagerBench with Pareto reporting and a
selected operating point---is delivered, and its budgeted-evaluation component became the main
contribution. Three deviations are argued from evidence: the neural bandit is unjustifiable in a
45-cell space where random search is competitive (the comparison is delivered by replay); the
internal HA metric plays the constraint role more faithfully than an external benchmark, since
ManagerBench items are genuine dilemmas without a single correct answer, with an optional
external HarmBench spot-check retained; and no GPU was available for a local white-box model, so
all experiments are black-box via API---the realistic deployment setting. Appendix~\ref{app:proposal}
records the full mapping.
